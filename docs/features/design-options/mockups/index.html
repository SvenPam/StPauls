Ten looks for the church website

Here are ten ways the website could look. Number 1 is the site as it is now. The others go from small tidy-ups to something completely new.

Tap a picture to open that design. The grey bar at the top lets you switch between the home page and a blog article, or step on to the next design. The words and photos are the real ones from the site. Only the home page and one article are filled in, so other links won't go anywhere.

It's worth looking on your phone as well as a computer.

When you've had a look

Let us know the two or three you like best, and anything you'd keep or avoid from any of them. Mixing bits from different designs is fine.

4Easy reading

Same colours, new layout

5Welcome first

The home page opens with what a visitor needs: service times, where to find the church and the next few Sundays. Notices and the blog follow.

Needs from you: Service times kept in Site settings, updated when they change. The Sundays list fills itself from those.

6Parish magazine

Laid out like the front page of the parish magazine: a masthead, the newest blog story large, other stories below and notices and service times down the side.

Needs from you: Works best when blog articles have a picture. Service times kept in Site settings.

Home pageArticle

7Signposts

No dropdown menus. The home page is a set of big, clear buttons to every part of the site, with notices you tap to open. Easy on a phone.

Needs from you: Nothing new.

Something new

New colours and a new layout. The words and photos stay the same.

8Churchyard

New colours from the churchyard photos: yew green, crocus purple and pale stone. A calmer page with more space, service times up front and a strip of church photos.

Needs from you: Service times kept in Site settings. Uses photos already on the site.

9Stained glass

Bold and colourful, taken from a stained glass window: ruby, sapphire, emerald and amber with dark lead lines. Each part of the site has its own colour, and photos sit in arched frames.

Needs from you: Service times kept in Site settings.

10Estuary

Quiet and modern, in colours from the Ribble estuary: slate blue, pale sky and sand. Big, light lettering, a wide photo and the key facts on one line like a sign board.
